Lesson 01

What Is a Takeoff?

A takeoff is the disciplined process of reading project drawings, identifying the required products, counting each applicable occurrence, and recording the final quantities for review.

Do not just count symbols. Understand what you are looking at first.

Lesson 02

Read the Drawing Key / Legend

Before counting anything, read the key. Confirm what each symbol means, which product or device it represents, its part number, the abbreviations in use, and which symbols belong in this takeoff.

Do not start counting until you understand the key.

Lesson 03

Read the Drawing Notes

Read all applicable notes immediately after the key. Notes can change how symbols are interpreted, including what is new or existing, what is excluded, and what relationships or requirements affect the count.

Key first. Notes second. Count third.

Lesson 04

Identify the Products

Make the product identity explicit before counting. Use the drawing key and verified Academy product information to establish the part number, description, and drawing symbol.

PART NUMBER | WHAT IS IT | DRAWING SYMBOL

Only use mappings that are established by the project documents or verified Academy sources.

Lesson 05

Apply the Color-Coding System

Use the designated color for each product type so the drawing is easier to scan and audit. The Takeoffs source is the visual reference for the manual color-coded process.

The source does not establish a readable color-to-product assignment here. Do not guess one; confirm it with Ban-Koe.

Lesson 06

Count Systematically

Start at a known location, work through one area in a consistent pattern, and continue until every applicable device has been considered exactly once.

Lesson 07

Cross Off What You Count

After identifying and counting an item, cross it off on the drawing. Use the physical mark instead of memory to prevent double-counting.

Identify → Count → Cross off → Move to the next item

Lesson 08

Tally the Quantities

Maintain a separate tally for each product or part number. The result must be clear as part number → total quantity.

Lesson 09

Enter the Takeoff Spreadsheet

When the drawing is completely counted, transfer the final quantities to the takeoff spreadsheet. The drawing is the source of the count; the spreadsheet is the organized final tally.

Lesson 10

Reconcile Your Takeoff

Compare the drawing markings, crossed-off items, tally, and spreadsheet quantity. If they do not match, return to the drawing and recount.

Drawing markings + crossed-off items + tally = spreadsheet quantity

Lesson 11

Common Takeoff Mistakes

  • Counting before reading the key or notes
  • Misidentifying symbols or mixing product types
  • Double-counting, missing devices, or forgetting to cross off
  • Entering the wrong part number or quantity
  • Failing to reconcile the final tally

Project

Small Office Fire Alarm Upgrade

System Type

Edwards iO64 with 48 addressable devices total (small-building fit; EST4 remains available when the project requires it)

Scope

Two-story office building with smoke detection, manual pull stations, notification appliances, duct detection, and HVAC shutdown interfaces.

Description

You are preparing a basic material takeoff for a small two-story office building with 48 addressable devices total. This scenario is intentionally sized so the Edwards iO64 is a valid small-building option because it supports up to 64 devices and its capacity cannot be expanded. EST4 remains available when the project requires a different platform.

Project Requirements

  • 1 fire alarm control panel
  • 2 floors
  • 10 smoke detectors per floor
  • First floor: 2 single-action manual pull stations and 1 double-action manual pull station
  • Second floor: 2 single-action manual pull stations
  • 4 wall-mounted low-frequency horn-strobes per floor
  • The notification drawing specifies G4LFV appliances on 4-inch octagon boxes
  • Separate ordinary wall horn-strobe schedule: 7 new red FIRE-marked locations
  • 1 duct smoke detector
  • The duct detector must provide a signal to the fire alarm system
  • The system must provide HVAC shutdown
  • Provide appropriate modules for the required interfaces
